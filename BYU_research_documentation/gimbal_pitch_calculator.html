Front-of-House Gimbal Pitch

Altitude is how high the drone flies. Run is the horizontal distance from the drone to the house, 5–100 ft in 5-ft steps. Target height is how far up the house's front face the shot is centered — drag it above the foot of the house to frame more of the building instead of the ground. Gimbal pitch uses the straight-down-is-90° convention: 90° is nadir, 0° is level with the horizon. Using the DJI Mini 4 Pro's vertical field of view, the diagram and table also show the vertical range each shot actually captures on the house's face — not just where the frame is centered. Switch Pitch to Manual to lock the gimbal at one angle and see how the frame shifts as the run changes — useful for picking one pitch that covers a whole range of street + setback distances.

The height slider below is capped at 30 ft — a placeholder for a typical two-story house. Tell me the real height (or a range) and I'll reset the cap so the slider's precision lands where you actually need it.
The altitude slider spans 20–300 ft, up to the Part 107 recreational/commercial ceiling of 400 ft AGL with room to spare. Target height's own ceiling always stays 5 ft below whatever altitude is set, since a shot centered at or above the drone's own height isn't a downward-tilted shot anymore.
Camera spec note: DJI publishes the Mini 4 Pro's FOV as a single 82.1° figure and a 24mm 35mm-equivalent focal length — neither states the physical sensor size or actual focal length needed for this math directly. The 6.72mm actual focal length below is sourced from real EXIF data reported by Mini 4 Pro owners; the sensor dimensions are derived from it and cross-checked two independent ways (see Equations). If you'd rather not rely on that, open any Mini 4 Pro photo's EXIF data yourself (FocalLength tag) and tell me what it says — that beats any secondhand number.

Equations
drop = alt − height The effective vertical drop from the drone down to the target point — the drone's full altitude, minus how far up the house that point sits.
θ = arctan(run ÷ drop) The top angle θ: inverse tangent of run over drop, measured from straight down.
pitch = 90° − θ Gimbal pitch in the straight-down-is-90° convention. Heads up: DJI Fly's own Gimbal Pitch field is negative for downward tilt — a pitch of 65° here is −65° in the app.
dist = √(drop² + run²) Straight-line distance from the drone to the target point, by the Pythagorean theorem.
halfFOV = arctan((sensorH ÷ 2) ÷ focal) Half the camera's vertical field of view, from its physical sensor height and focal length (pinhole model). For the Mini 4 Pro: sensorH ≈ 7.27mm, focal ≈ 6.72mm → halfFOV ≈ 28.4°. Both figures are derived, not read directly off a DJI spec sheet — see the camera-spec note above.
θedge = θ ± halfFOV The top and bottom rays of the frame sit halfFOV on either side of the center sight line, in the same straight-down-referenced angle as θ itself.
frame height = alt − (run ÷ tan θedge) Each ray's own height on the house, using the same drop → height relationship as the center point — just solved at the frame's edges instead of its middle.
center ht = alt − run · tan(pitch) Manual mode: where the middle of the frame lands on the house for a fixed pitch. The frame edges use the same formula above with θ = 90° − pitch.
target pos = 50% + 50% · tan(θtarget − θ) ÷ tan(halfFOV) Manual mode: where the target height sits in the photo, 0% at the bottom edge and 100% at the top (pinhole projection). 50% means it's dead center; outside 0–100% means it's out of the shot.
